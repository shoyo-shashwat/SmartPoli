    How this works
    What the twin looks at

    Every 15 minutes: the sugar so far and how fast it is changing, when the last meal and insulin were, the time of day, and facts about the patient such as age and diabetes drugs.

    How well it works

    Checked on patients it had never seen. Sugar forecasts were off by about 5 mg/dL at 15 minutes and 17 mg/dL at 60 minutes, better than assuming sugar stays the same (6.5 and 20.0). The low alert warned about 85 to 100 of every 100 lows at 1 to 2 alerts a day (development results; the final test comes last).

    What it cannot do
    • It is a risk flag, not a diagnosis.
    • It learned from 100 patients in one hospital.
    • Sensor errors can cause false alarms.
    • How it reacts to missed doses is untested.

    Model demo-0.1. Trained on ShanghaiT2DM (Zhao et al., 2023). Risk flag for the doctor, not a diagnosis.
