Twin tab: four ways to show the same model

Every option has the same demo patient, the same replay slider and the same "what happens next" actions (note, medicine timeline, report, mark as reviewed, snooze) with an activity log. Only the way the forecast is shown differs. Try the replay slider to move from calm to watch to alert.

1. Chart (first prototype)

Line chart with a forecast band. Most precise, but the doctor has to interpret a curve.

Best for: a second look, or a cardiologist or diabetologist who reads CGM curves daily.

Open
2. Briefing (A)

One sentence answer first, three reason pills, a tiny sparkline, then what to do.

Best for: understanding in five seconds on a phone.

Open
3. Next two hours (B)

Eight colour blocks for the next 2 hours plus a short story of how we got here. No curve.

Best for: seeing when the risk starts, at a glance.

Open
4. Worklist (C)

A list of patients sorted by urgency with a detail panel for the selected one.

Best for: a doctor with many patients; shows the twin working as a triage tool.

Open
Recommendation

Combine them. Open on the worklist (C), because a doctor's real question is "who needs me now". The selected patient opens as the briefing (A): a one-sentence answer, the two-hour strip (B), then the next-step actions. Put the line chart behind a "Show the curve" button for the doctors who want the detail. This gives a first-time viewer the answer in seconds and still lets an expert check the model.
