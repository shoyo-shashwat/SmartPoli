How this model works
What it looks at

Every 15 minutes it looks at the sugar so far: the current value, how fast it is changing, and the lowest and highest in the last hour. It also looks at when the last meal and insulin dose were, the time of day, and facts about the patient such as age, diabetes drugs and kidney function.

What it predicts

Sugar over the next 2 hours with a likely range, and the chance of a low or a high. Low sugar is shown as a level (calm, watch, alert) because real lows are rare and a single percentage would suggest more precision than we have.

How it was checked

It learned from other patients and was scored on patients it had never seen. Results so far, on development patients (the final locked test comes last):

For lows, the forecast warned about 85 to 100 of every 100 low events at 1 to 2 alerts per patient per day (66 events, 24 patients).

What it cannot do
  • It is a risk flag for the doctor, not a diagnosis or a treatment change.
  • It learned from 100 patients in one Chinese hospital, so it may be less accurate for others.
  • A sensor that reads too low can create false alarms. The model corrects for each patient's usual offset when it learns.
  • Missed doses are shown on the timeline, but how well the model reacts to them is untested.

Model demo-0.1. Trained on the ShanghaiT2DM dataset (Zhao et al., Scientific Data 2023). Risk flag for the doctor, not a diagnosis.
